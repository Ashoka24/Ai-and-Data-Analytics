Research Review Checklist

Before using a document-based answer, identify the original source and confirm its publication date, authorship, methods and limitations. For scientific claims, distinguish a primary study from a review, editorial, preprint or secondary summary.

Check whether the cited passage directly supports the claim. A citation that mentions the same topic is not sufficient if it does not support the conclusion. Look for missing context, uncertainty, contradictory evidence and limitations described elsewhere in the document.

Separate what a source reports from what an analyst infers. Correlation does not establish causation, and a result from one population or experimental setup may not generalize to another. Pay attention to sample size, inclusion criteria, controls, measurement methods and statistical uncertainty where these details are available.

When multiple sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and compare the methods and context instead of forcing a single conclusion. Record the exact source and page so another reader can reproduce the check.

A document assistant is a discovery and navigation aid. It does not replace reading the full paper, checking supplementary materials, or consulting a qualified subject-matter expert for consequential decisions.
